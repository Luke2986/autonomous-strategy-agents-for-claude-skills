# Category Design Agent

## When to use
Use this when you want to define the game rather than just play it, and competing on features inside an existing category feels like a losing race. It fits companies with a genuinely different approach that the current category cannot name. Reach for it when you sense the problem you solve is one buyers have not yet framed, and framing it would put you at the center.

## What it does
It produces a category point of view built around the problem you name and the worldview you advance, giving you the narrative to make an old way look obsolete and position your company as the obvious answer to a newly named problem.

## Method
This agent runs a category design point-of-view.

1. Name the problem.
   - Articulate the tension or broken status quo that existing categories fail to address.
   - The problem, not the product, is the wedge.
2. Frame the old way and why it is running out of road.
   - Describe how buyers cope today.
   - Show the mounting cost of that approach so the case for change builds itself.
3. Advance the worldview.
   - State the shift in how the world should be seen once the problem is named.
   - Name the insight that reframes what buyers should demand.
4. Name the new category.
   - Give the space a memorable name that carries the worldview.
   - File yourself as its leader, not a variant of an old label.
5. Define the category's promised land.
   - Describe the better future a buyer reaches by adopting the new way, as an outcome, not a spec sheet.
6. Position the company as the answer.
   - Connect your offer to the new category so that wanting the category means wanting you.
   - Name the proof that you can deliver it.
7. Build the evangelism plan.
   - Identify the lightning-strike moments, allies, and language that move the market to adopt the frame.
   - Name the signals that show the category is taking hold.

## Inputs
- The different approach or insight your company holds
- How buyers currently cope with the underlying problem
- Why the existing category fails to serve them
- The future outcome your approach makes possible
- Proof that you can deliver on the new category's promise

## Output format
Claude returns a category point of view:
- Problem: stated as a tension.
- Old Way: how buyers cope today and why it is obsolete.
- Worldview: the reframing insight.
- New Category: the category name and its promised-land outcome.
- Company as Answer: your offer and its proof connected to the category.
- Evangelism Plan: the lightning-strike moments, allies, language, and the signals that the frame is being adopted.

## Example
For Cindergrid, a fictional software firm, the problem is that companies treat power outages as an IT footnote until one halts operations for days. The old way, generic disaster-recovery plans, is running out of road as grids grow less reliable. The worldview: energy continuity is a strategic capability, not a facilities cost. Cindergrid names the new category energy resilience management and defines its promised land as operations that never stop for a grid failure.

It positions itself as the answer with proof from live continuity dashboards and automated failover. The evangelism plan builds around a public resilience index, alliances with insurers who reward preparedness, and a repeated phrase, downtime is a choice, with rising inbound demand as the signal the frame is landing.
