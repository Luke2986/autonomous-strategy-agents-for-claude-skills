# Innovation Thesis Agent

## When to use

Use this agent when innovation spend lacks a clear where-to-play logic and
teams chase whatever looks exciting this quarter. It fits when a lab,
venture arm, or product group needs boundaries on where it will and will
not invest. Reach for it before committing an innovation budget or
greenlighting a new wave of bets.

## What it does

Produces an innovation thesis that states where the organization will play,
what it believes will change, and which zones are explicitly off-limits.
The output gives teams a filter to say a confident no, so the yes bets get
real resources.

## Method

This agent runs a where-to-play innovation thesis.

1. State the strategic intent. Name what innovation is meant to achieve
   for the business: defend the core, extend into adjacencies, or open a
   new horizon.

2. Write the belief. Articulate the point of view on how the market,
   technology, or customer behavior will shift, since a thesis is a bet on
   change, not a list of ideas.

3. Define the where-to-play boundaries. Name the customer needs,
   technologies, and business models inside the arena, and the ones
   deliberately outside it.

4. Set the right-to-win logic. Explain why this organization, not a rival,
   is positioned to win in the chosen arena, drawing on assets, data,
   channels, or capabilities.

5. Choose focus areas. Select two to four themes inside the arena where
   effort concentrates, each tied to the belief and the right-to-win.

6. Draw the no-go zones. State explicitly what will not be pursued, even if
   attractive, because it sits outside the thesis or lacks a right-to-win.
   This is the discipline that makes the thesis real.

7. Attach validation gates. For each focus area, name the assumption that
   must prove true and the signal that would kill or scale the bet.

## Inputs

- The strategic intent behind innovating: defend, extend, or open new

- Your view of how the market or technology is shifting

- The assets and capabilities that give a right-to-win

- The ideas or areas currently competing for investment

- Budget and risk appetite for innovation

## Output format

Claude returns a Strategic Intent line, a Belief statement of the change
being bet on, and a Where To Play section naming what is inside and outside
the arena in prose. It then gives a Right To Win rationale, two to four
Focus Areas each tied to the belief, an explicit No-Go Zones list, and
Validation Gates pairing each focus area with the assumption to test and
the kill-or-scale signal.

## Example

For a fictional company, Meridian Insurance:

Strategic Intent. Extend into adjacencies.

Belief. Small businesses will buy coverage embedded at the point of another
purchase rather than from an agent.

Where To Play. Embedded insurance for online marketplaces; not consumer
auto, which stays with the core.

Right To Win. An existing underwriting engine and API.

Focus Areas. Marketplace partnerships and instant underwriting.

No-Go Zones. Standalone consumer apps and any line requiring a new agent
force.

Validation Gate. The partnership bet scales only if attach rate clears a
set threshold in the first pilot.
