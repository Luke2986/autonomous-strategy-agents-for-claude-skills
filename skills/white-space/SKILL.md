# White Space Agent

## When to use
Use this when you are hunting for uncontested demand and the obvious market is crowded. It fits the search for growth beyond the core, when incremental share gains are expensive and you suspect real opportunity sits in needs no one serves well. Reach for it before a new-product cycle or an expansion bet, to aim the search at gaps rather than at rivals.

## What it does
It produces a white-space map of attractive unmet needs, showing where demand exists that current offers ignore, ranked so you can tell a real opportunity from an empty quadrant.

## Method
This agent runs a white-space and unmet-need mapping analysis.

1. Frame the space.
   - Choose two axes that define the field.
   - Typically customer segments or jobs on one axis, and needs, occasions, or use contexts on the other.
2. Plot the incumbents.
   - Place existing offers, yours and rivals', onto the grid.
   - Reveal where competition clusters and where cells sit empty.
3. Populate the empty cells with candidate unmet needs.
   - Draw from customer frustrations, workarounds, non-consumption, and adjacent behaviors.
4. Qualify each gap.
   - A true white space needs real demand, weak or absent supply, and a plausible path for you to serve it.
   - Discard cells that are empty for a good reason.
5. Score the surviving gaps on attractiveness.
   - Size and growth of the latent demand.
   - Intensity of the unmet need and fit with your capabilities.
6. Assess reachability.
   - What you would have to build, borrow, or change to serve the gap.
   - How fast a rival could follow.
7. Rank and recommend.
   - Order the white spaces by attractiveness and reachability.
   - Name which to test first and the cheapest experiment that would confirm or kill the demand.

## Inputs
- The market or category you want to search
- Customer segments, jobs, or use occasions you know
- Existing offers from you and competitors
- Signals of frustration, workarounds, or non-consumption
- Your capabilities and where you could stretch

## Output format
Claude returns a white-space map in prose:
- Frame: the two axes in scope.
- Map: where incumbents cluster and which cells sit empty.
- Candidate White Spaces: one card per qualified gap describing the unmet need, the latent demand, the state of current supply, and the fit with your capabilities.
- Ranking: by attractiveness and reachability.
- Test Plan: which gap to probe first and the cheapest experiment to validate it.

## Example
For Brightwell Dental, a fictional chain of clinics, the agent frames patient life-stage against care occasion. Incumbents cluster around routine adult check-ups and emergency pain. An empty cell appears at anxious first-time patients seeking gentle, unhurried onboarding, a need served today only by workarounds and word of mouth. It qualifies: demand is real, supply is thin, and the capability fit is close.

It scores well on attractiveness because anxious patients churn expensively and pay for reassurance, and reachability is high since it needs process and training rather than new equipment. The map ranks it above a costlier cosmetic-teens gap. The test plan launches a gentle-onboarding pilot at two clinics, measuring conversion from first visit to a booked plan as the signal.
