# Differentiation Agent

## When to use
Use this when the offer looks the same as every competitor and buyers cannot tell you apart. It fits crowded categories where feature lists converge and price becomes the only lever left. Reach for it before you cut price by default, because the real problem is usually undifferentiated positioning rather than being too expensive.

## What it does
It produces a clear split between your points of difference and your points of parity, with a defensibility test on each difference, so you know what genuinely sets you apart and whether that gap can survive a competitor's response.

## Method
This agent runs a points-of-difference versus points-of-parity analysis.

1. Fix the frame.
   - Name the category and the specific rivals buyers weigh you against.
   - Difference only means something against a defined set.
2. List points of parity.
   - Name the attributes you must have simply to be considered, the table stakes.
   - Confirm you actually meet them, since a missing parity point sinks you before difference matters.
3. List candidate points of difference.
   - Name where you are genuinely better, different, or unique against the frame.
4. Filter each candidate on three tests.
   - Valued: does the target buyer care.
   - Distinct: do rivals lack it.
   - True: can you deliver and prove it.
   - Drop any candidate that fails one test.
5. Score defensibility for each surviving difference.
   - How easily can a competitor copy, buy, or neutralize it.
   - What protects it: cost position, network effects, switching costs, brand, or proprietary assets.
6. Rank the differences.
   - Order by combined buyer value and defensibility.
   - Separate durable advantages from fragile ones.
7. Recommend the investment.
   - Where to widen a durable gap.
   - Which fragile differences to shore up or retire, and which parity gaps to close first.

## Inputs
- Your product and the rivals buyers compare it against
- The features and attributes of both you and competitors
- What your target buyer values most
- Any assets that make an advantage hard to copy
- Where you currently win and lose deals

## Output format
Claude returns a differentiation analysis:
- Frame: the category and rivals in scope.
- Points of Parity: the table stakes and whether you meet them.
- Points of Difference: one card per surviving difference describing its buyer value, its distinctiveness, its truth, and a defensibility verdict.
- Ranking: durable advantages separated from fragile ones.
- Recommendations: gaps to widen, fragile points to shore up or retire, and parity gaps to close.

## Example
For Tempo Bikes, a fictional e-bike maker, the frame is mid-priced commuter e-bikes. Parity points are range, motor power, and warranty, all of which Tempo meets. Three candidate differences survive the filter. A swappable battery network is valued, distinct, and true, and scores high on defensibility because the charging-station partnerships are hard to replicate quickly. A lightweight frame is valued and true but weakly defensible, since rivals can source the same alloy. A companion app is true but not distinct, so it drops to parity.

The ranking makes the battery network the durable advantage and the frame a fragile one. Recommendation: invest to widen the battery-network lead through exclusive station deals, treat the frame as temporary, and stop pitching the app as a differentiator.

A sample point-of-difference card reads:
- Difference: swappable battery network.
- Buyer value: high, since it removes range anxiety for commuters.
- Distinctiveness: no mid-priced rival offers comparable coverage.
- Truth: proven by the live station footprint.
- Defensibility: strong, protected by exclusive charging-station partnerships.
