# Brand Positioning Agent

## When to use
Use this when the brand does not own a clear place in the buyer's mind and every attempt to describe it slides into category boilerplate. It fits the moment sales, marketing, and product each answer "what are we" differently. Reach for it before a rebrand, a raise, or a launch, when one crisp answer must anchor everything downstream.

## What it does
It produces a positioning statement built from a target, a frame of reference, a point of difference, and proof, giving the brand a single defensible claim to the ground it wants to own.

## Method
This agent runs the positioning statement framework.

1. Define the target.
   - Name the specific buyer whose mind you want to win.
   - Describe them by need and context, not demographics alone.
2. Set the frame of reference.
   - Name the category or alternative the buyer mentally files you under.
   - Choose it deliberately, since the frame decides who you are compared against.
3. Name the point of difference.
   - Identify the one thing you do better or differently that the target values.
   - Confirm rivals cannot easily match it.
4. Assemble the proof.
   - Gather reasons to believe the point of difference from capabilities, evidence, or design, not adjectives.
5. Write the statement in the canonical form.
   - For the target, brand is the frame that point of difference, because proof.
6. Stress-test the statement.
   - Is it true, is it relevant to the target, is it distinct from the frame, and is it durable against an obvious competitor response.
   - Rewrite until all four hold.
7. Derive the immediate implications.
   - What the positioning demands of product, pricing, and message.
   - What it rules out.

## Inputs
- The target buyer and the need or context that defines them
- The category or alternatives buyers compare you against
- What you genuinely do better or differently
- Evidence or capabilities that back the difference
- Competitors whose positioning you must stand apart from

## Output format
Claude returns a positioning package:
- Building Blocks: Target, Frame of Reference, Point of Difference, and Proof, each stated in a sentence or two.
- Positioning Statement: the canonical single-sentence form.
- Stress Test: a short verdict on truth, relevance, distinctiveness, and durability.
- Implications: what the positioning demands of product, price, and message and what it rules out.

## Example
For Pinehold Boots, a fictional workwear brand, the target is tradespeople who ruin cheap boots every season and distrust fashion labels. The frame of reference is durable work boots, not lifestyle footwear, which sets the comparison against rugged rivals rather than sneakers. The point of difference is a resole-for-life guarantee that halves lifetime cost. The proof is a stitched, resolable construction and a free resole program.

The statement reads: for tradespeople who wear out boots fast, Pinehold is the work boot you resole instead of replace, because every pair is built to be rebuilt. The stress test holds on all four counts. Implications: pricing can sit above disposable rivals, the resole promise must lead every message, and cheap glued construction is ruled out.

The building blocks behind that statement read:
- Target: tradespeople who ruin cheap boots each season and distrust fashion labels.
- Frame of reference: durable work boots, not lifestyle footwear.
- Point of difference: a resole-for-life guarantee that halves lifetime cost.
- Proof: stitched, resolable construction and a free resole program.
- Stress test: true, relevant, distinct, and durable against a glued-boot rival.
