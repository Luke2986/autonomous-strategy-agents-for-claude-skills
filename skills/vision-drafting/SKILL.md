# Vision Drafting Agent

## When to use
Use this agent when the organization needs a destination worth committing to.
It fits the moment when the strategy has priorities and plans but no clear picture of where it is all heading.
Reach for it at the start of a new strategic era, after a major pivot, or when the current vision is generic, forgettable, or indistinguishable from a competitor's.

## What it does
It produces a vision statement with a long-horizon ambition.
That is a short, vivid statement of the future the organization is building toward, backed by the ambition and the reasoning that make it credible and motivating.
It also shows the through-line from today's strengths, so the vision reads as reachable rather than detached.

## Method
The agent frames a long-horizon ambition into a committing vision.

1. Set the horizon.
   - Choose a time frame far enough out to be aspirational, typically five to ten years or more.
   - The vision should describe a destination, not a target.
2. Clarify the core.
   - Surface what the organization uniquely stands for.
   - Name the change it wants to create in the world of its customers or market.
3. Define the ambition.
   - State the bold end-state: the position or impact the organization intends to reach.
   - It should stretch beyond the current trajectory without becoming fantasy.
4. Draft candidate statements.
   - Write two or three vision options, each short, concrete, and vivid enough to picture.
   - Strip out jargon and hedging.
5. Test each candidate against four checks.
   - Aspirational, clear, memorable, and distinctive to this organization rather than any peer.
6. Refine and recommend.
   - Sharpen the strongest candidate and state the ambition and reasoning behind it.
7. Connect to today.
   - Trace the through-line from current strengths to the vision so it reads as reachable.

## Inputs
- What the organization does and what it uniquely stands for
- The time horizon for the vision
- Leadership's aspiration and any non-negotiable values
- An existing vision or mission to build from, if any
- The audience the vision must land with: employees, customers, investors

## Output format
Claude returns the result in this shape:
- Two or three candidate vision statements, each a single vivid sentence.
- A test note scoring each candidate against aspirational, clear, memorable, and distinctive.
- The recommended statement, named explicitly.
- The long-horizon ambition behind it, in a short paragraph.
- A through-line linking today's strengths to that future, in prose.

## Example
Take Terra Farms, a fictional vertical-farming startup.
Three candidates are drafted, tested, and narrowed to one: "Fresh produce grown within a mile of every city plate."
The ambition behind it: to make hyper-local, year-round growing the default for urban leafy greens within a decade, displacing long-haul supply chains.
It passes the four checks: aspirational (it redefines the category), clear (anyone can picture it), memorable (short and concrete), and distinctive (few rivals frame proximity this way).
The through-line notes Terra's current strength in low-energy growing modules as the credible bridge from today to that future.
The discarded candidates were dropped for hedged wording and for describing a benefit any grower could claim.
